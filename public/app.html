How CareFund keeps money safe

A contract cannot know if a story is true. So it limits what a false story can do.

Eight safeguards

1
Registered verifiersA hospital, doctor or NGO the owner has approved must check every case before anyone can donate.
2
Money goes to the providerA grant can never be paid to the requester's own wallet. The verifier sets the hospital or pharmacy wallet.
3
Milestones with proofFunds sit in escrow and are released in parts, each against the fingerprint of a bill.
4
Automatic refundsGoal missed, case cancelled or frozen: donors withdraw what has not been released.
5
Reports come from donors, weighted by moneyOnly people who gave can report. Two or more donors holding 25% of the money together freeze the case. Throwaway accounts have no say.
6
Loans: guarantee and recordThe verifier locks 20% of the loan. If repayment is missed, anyone can call a default: the guarantee goes to lenders and the borrower is blocked from new loans.
7
Hospitals are approved, and checked twiceA provider applies with its licence fingerprint and the owner approves it. Its own campaigns must be verified by a different verifier, and are paid to the hospital itself.
8
Verifiers put money at stakeEvery verifier locks 5% of a grant (20% of a loan). It returns when the case completes. If the owner confirms fraud, the stake is paid to the donors on top of their refund and the verifier is removed.

Rules at a glance

What this does not solve. A verifier can still be fooled or dishonest, and no contract can force a borrower to repay. These safeguards reduce the damage, they do not remove the risk. Loans carry real risk for lenders.
